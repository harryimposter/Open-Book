1 · What this project is

Open Book is a private-bank investment-desk simulator with a two-sided premise: a Solutions desk that manufactures and curates a daily trade-idea feed, and an Advisor who consumes it against a live book of eight clients. It runs as one page — index.html + openbook.js — with two header views (Advisor · Solutions) and two tabs (Idea Feed · Advisor Book), plus a light/dark theme, a "Search everything" ⌘K command palette, and an embedded legacy app for the deep client pages. The point of the project is not the ideas themselves — it is the discipline around them: every idea is scored by a published rubric, mapped to clients by a transparent engine that reads their real holdings, dated honestly by a fingerprint ledger, and sourced under a two-source rule with citation integrity enforced by a validator.

The two-axis idea philosophy. "How good is the idea" (conviction) and "how right is it for this client" (client fit) are deliberately separate scores computed by separate engines. A brilliant idea can be wrong for a book; a mediocre idea can be exactly what a gap needs. Conflating them is the cardinal sin the project is built to avoid.

2 · Design principles

  1. Derived, never hand-picked. Idea→client links are computed live against the current books. No hand-curated "recommended for" lists that drift out of sync with reality.
  2. No black boxes. Every score decomposes into visible, named components — the mapping axes open on click with per-axis notes; conviction pillars carry one-line reads; goal derivation lists its drivers in plain English.
  3. Facts are tagged. Every datapoint in an idea is tagged sourced (≥2 independent sources) or estimated. A fact that can't be verified is labelled, not laundered.
  4. Citation integrity is a hard rule. Never attribute a view to a named author whose content was not actually read during the sweep. Every view-citation carries a basis field stating what was read and when; the validator rejects view-citations without one. An owned original call (ownView: true) beats a borrowed authority every time.
  5. Honest dates. An idea's "posted" date only moves when its argument changes (fingerprint ledger). A no-op re-commit never makes a stale idea look fresh.
  6. Charts are market data or they don't ship. Any chart without a fetched, provenance-stamped series is stripped by the validator. No hand-drawn "indicative" paths.
  7. Attribution discipline. Don't blame the scariest headline for a move — name the verified driver. Big single-name earnings (a Samsung, an IBM) are flagged as drivers where they actually moved the tape.

3 · Architecture

A static site with no build step: open index.html or serve the folder (python -m http.server 5544). All state is in checked-in JS/JSON files plus localStorage for drafted views. Nothing else runs server-side — which is what makes the whole thing deployable as a free Vercel static site and auditable file-by-file.

5 · Goal derivation (goals.js)

Real clients never hand you a target allocation — they say "I want to grow capital", "I have a $19m mortgage", "I need $0.8m/yr". deriveGoals(client) infers a three-bucket goal vector — Growth · Income · Preservation — from both sides of the balance sheet plus revealed risk appetite, in the spirit of Chhabra's risk-allocation framework: fund the floor first, then deploy the surplus by how hard the money must work.

Inputs (all measured off the client object)

  • Horizon — parsed from the free-text horizon ("Drawdown · 0–5 yrs" → phase=drawdown, H=2.5y; "Long-term · 7–10 yrs" → accumulation, H=8.5y).
  • Funding goal — parsed from the headline: a value target ("grow to $75m by 2034") becomes a required return = (target/current)^(1/years) − 1; an income target ("fund $3.6m/yr") becomes an income need as % of AUM; both can coexist.
  • Liabilities — near-term bills (tax, settlements) as % of AUM; debt load; annual debt service (rate defaults to 6%).
  • Willingness — blended 50/50 from stated (parsed from the risk string: aggressive 0.95, growth 0.80, moderate 0.50, conservative 0.30) and revealed (read off the book: 0.5 × risk-asset share + 0.3 × low-defensiveness + 0.2 × single-name concentration appetite). If nothing is stated, revealed stands alone.

The scoring (each bucket = sum of named, inspectable terms)

Preservation = 8 (base) + 1.0·nearBill% + 0.3·debtLoad% + 1.0·max(0, topName% − 15) + 15 (if drawdown phase) + 3·max(0, 5 − H) + 28·(1 − willingness) Income = 8 (base) + 8·incomeNeed% + 8·debtService% + 0.6·debtLoad% + 15 (if drawdown phase) + 18·(1 − willingness) Growth = 8 (base) + 5·requiredReturn% + 3·max(0, H − 6) + willingness × (30 if a required-return signal exists, else 45) vector = largest-remainder normalisation of the three raw scores to exactly 100
Three subtleties that make it work. (1) Anti-circularity: the book sets the willingness scalar, never the target vector — the target is still built by the bucket logic, so the gap between "have" and "need" stays meaningful. (2) Bidirectional willingness: a low appetite must add ballast (the caution terms), not merely shrink Growth — otherwise a cautious client with no liabilities comes out growth-heavy by default. (3) Confidence reweight: with no goal signal, the willingness coefficient rises from 30 to 45 — a missing written goal must never quietly de-risk an obviously aggressive book.

The result carries a source ("stated-goal" 0.9 confidence · "stated-risk" 0.7 · "revealed" 0.55), the plain-English driver list surfaced in the UI ("Needs ~7.1%/yr over 8y → Growth", "$19m debt load → Income/Preservation matching"), and every component number. A manual goalOverride pins a vector and short-circuits the model. The current book is folded into the same three buckets for the "now vs goal" bar: equity + directional alternatives → Growth; fixed income, credit, real assets → Income; cash, gold, uncorrelated alternatives → Preservation; structured notes by purpose (keyword-classified: buffer/protect → Preservation, autocall/coupon → Income, else Growth).

6 · The idea→client mapping engine (mapping.js)

scoreIdeaForClient(idea, client) is the single scorer — the Idea Feed's flagged clients, the Top 3 breadth ranking, the suitability flowchart, advisor-draft mapping and the legacy pages all funnel through it, so no two tabs can ever disagree. It reads sector-level signals off the reconciled book: sector exposure, the 24-month sector history, in-sector holdings, and the parsed mandate.

The four axes (each 0–100, flat weights summing to 1.00)

AxisWeightComputation
Affinity fit (holdings)0.294max(0, ThematicAffinity − ConcentrationPenalty). Affinity = recency-weighted (λ=0.94) percentile of the current sector allocation within the client's 24-month sector history — "is this a market they demonstrably live in?". Penalty = pp over the sector-comfort peg × 10 (capped 100). Comfort pegs by the asset's goal bucket: Growth 25 · Income 15 · Preservation 12 (% of book), with optional per-sector overrides.
Mandate & Risk0.2940.6 × RiskSuitability + 0.4 × IntentFit. RiskSuitability = deterministic matrix of the idea's riskProfile {vol, beta, structured} vs the client's mandate class (growth mandates reward high-beta; income mandates reward low-vol; preservation punishes high-beta). IntentFit = matrix mandate × goalType (growth↔appreciation 90, income↔yield 90, preservation↔protection 92, off-goal pairings lower).
Gap fit0.235Bucket-aware headroom: max(0, (bucketTarget − bucketCurrent)/bucketTarget × 100) where bucketTarget is the client's derived goal for the idea's bucket and bucketCurrent the book's current allocation to it. Zero once the goal bucket is full — a gold idea scores 0 for a client whose Preservation is already at target. Falls back to sector headroom vs the comfort peg only when no goal target exists.
Concentration within sector0.176raw = (1 − HHI) × 100 over the book's holdings inside the idea's sector (one name → 0; five equal names → 80). Inverted for fit by default: a concentrated sector needs a new name more, so fitContribution = 100 − raw. No in-sector holdings → neutral 50.

Combine — gate × bracket × goals multiplier

bracketFit = Σ (axisScore × weight) # 0–100 alignedFit = bracketFit × goalAlignment.mult # 0.85–1.15 (goals.js) fit = tradability × alignedFit # binary MiFID gate tiers: ≥66 Strong · ≥48 Good · else Marginal applies: tradable AND alignedFit ≥ 45 · flagged: fit ≥ 50, top 6
  • The goal-alignment multiplier (goals.js): an idea that fills a bucket where the client is under their derived goal is boosted (up to ×1.15); one piling into an over-goal bucket is dampened (down to ×0.85). mult = clamp(1 + gap/200).
  • The tradability gate (binary, computed before everything): Retail client + an OTC natural expression (collar, forward, OTC option — keyword taxonomy in data.js) → fit 0, suppressed. The UI surfaces suppressed clients with the MiFID reason rather than silently dropping them. Structured notes (autocalls, buffered notes) are packaged securities Retail can hold — the complexity check runs structured-keywords before OTC-keywords so "HALO basket" resolves as a note, not a basket.
What deliberately moved out of client fit. House-view fit is an idea-level property, not a per-client signal — it double-counted holdings already captured by Affinity and Gap — so it lives in the conviction rubric now. Tradability used to be an axis term (a non-tradable idea could still score ~75); as a global gate it zeroes the whole fit instead.

7 · Conviction scoring — two rubrics by idea kind

Computed by build_today_focus.py, never hand-entered. Pillar scores and data-quality tags are authored in the research payload; the generator stamps labels and maxima, sums, scales to /100 and bands. Each pillar's core input carries dq ∈ {sourced, estimated, unverified}.

Earnings ideas — the print rubric (4 pillars × 0–2, /8)

PillarScores 2 / 1 / 0
Asymmetry signalImplied straddle move ÷ average absolute realised move over the trailing 4 prints: materially below 1 (market underpricing the event) → 2 · near fair → 1 · implied rich → 0. Wide estimate dispersion can also carry it.
Sell-side consensusMajority buy + upside to mean PT + positive 30-day revisions: all three → 2 · partial → 1 · none/contradicts → 0.
Catalyst clarityPre-print: a dated, unpriced catalyst. Post-print: a reaction disproportionate to print quality. Clear → 2 · soft → 1 · none → 0.
Positioning & sentimentShort interest >10% of float OR an extreme setup → 2 · notable lean → 1 · neutral/unavailable → 0.

Banding: High = raw ≥6, no zero pillar, all inputs sourced · High — data gap = same but ≥1 estimated input · Medium = raw 4–5, or any zero pillar, or consensus unverified · Low = raw <4 or two-plus zeros (excluded from the board).

Ex-earnings ideas — the seven-pillar model (/15)

Pillar (max)What it scores
Gap / asymmetry (3)Thesis expected move ÷ the name's normal move over the horizon: ≥2.0 → 3 · 1.5–2.0 → 2 · 1.0–1.5 → 1 · <1.0 → 0. The target is a soft input → tagged estimated.
Catalyst (2)A dated, hard reason it moves soon → 2 · soft/undated → 1 · none → 0.
Consensus + confirmation (2)Either sell-side aligned OR a defensible variant view, AND an independent signal (breakout / flows / skew). Both legs → 2 · one → 1 · neither → 0.
Fundamental thesis (2)Two 0/1 checks: driver specificity (named, quantified drivers vs narrative) and variant view (an articulated, computable gap vs consensus, authored in the idea's variant {street, us, gap}). Pure consensus-hugging scores 0. dq is sourced only when drivers trace to cited facts.
House-view fit (2)Core desk theme, direct expression → 2 · on-theme/adjacent → 1 · off-theme or against → 0.
Positioning (2)Confirmation/veto, not a driver: a hard read (SI, CoT, flows) + a sentiment read (RSI at a crowded extreme). Crowd offside our trade → 2 · neutral/mixed → 1 · crowd with us (unwind risk) → 0.
Technical (2)Confirmation/veto: 50/100/200-day MA alignment, Bollinger entry, Fibonacci vs the thesis. Agrees → 2 · mixed → 1 · contradicts → 0.

score = raw ÷ 15 × 100 · High ≥75 · Medium ≥55 · Watch ≥0. The split is deliberately ~70/30: thesis-bearing pillars carry 11/15, positioning + technicals 4/15 — the view drives; technicals confirm or veto but cannot manufacture conviction.

The data-quality cap. If any pillar's core input is estimated or unverified, the ex-earnings label cannot exceed Medium (earnings shows "High — data gap"). This is the honest-data flywheel: wiring in a sourced feed (as tv_technicals.py does for the Technical pillar) visibly unlocks higher labels. Every idea also carries a changeMyMind field — the condition that would invalidate the view — surfaced on the tile but deliberately not scored (a kill-switch pillar would bias the model toward dated catalyst trades over strategic holds).

8 · The daily sweep — process and rules

The sweep is run on demand by a Claude Code session (no scheduler is wired up; the cadence is a convention). It is a whole-market sweep, not a US-equity screen:

Coverage spec (validator-enforced warnings)

  • Asset classes: equities, rates, credit, FX, commodities (+ structured where it fits); regions: US, Europe, UK, Asia.
  • FX and rates must be represented every run — the two most-often-missed classes.
  • The earnings book must carry both stances: pre-position (into a future print) and post-print (playing an already-reported reaction with a verified, sourced result).
  • At least one tactical idea — a short-dated option trade with a concrete construction (strikes/tenor/premium) and a defined entry/target/stop in a levels block — primarily in FX.
  • Dislocation scan: check large-cap drawdowns — a >~15% pullback in a widely-held name is a candidate idea, not noise.

Source rules

  • ≥2 independent sources for any stated fact; single-sourced figures are tagged estimated. Forward events stay future-tense.
  • Named authors, two tiers. Core (read every sweep): Citrini Research (thematic), Serenity, TSCS, Brent Donnelly (FX), Cem Karsan (vol/dealer positioning), Joseph Wang / Fed Guy (rates & Fed plumbing), SemiAnalysis (AI capex/semis). Conditional (read when an idea touches their domain — the natural second source): The Transcript (earnings), Benn Eifert / Moontower / Tier1 Alpha (options & vol), Fabricated Knowledge (semis), Rory Johnston / Doomberg (commodities), HighYield Harry (credit), The Market Ear / Brad Setser (flows), Andy Constan / Joseph Politano (rates & macro data).
  • A view-citation (kind "view") requires a basis: what was read, when, and what was taken from it. Fresh commentary that merely restates an unchanged thesis is not a reason to cite or re-date an idea.
  • Nothing bypasses the rubric or the mapping: an author's read informs conviction; it never auto-promotes an idea.

What a sweep actually writes

The sweep overwrites today_focus.json: an asOf date, a sweep block (source list, the rule line, a three-part brief — summary, watch bullets, full narrative paragraphs — and a desk note that archives what changed on the board and why, including what was held back and the attribution discipline line), an earnings array and an exEarnings array of idea objects (§9), and a meta block stamping the technicals feed as-of. Ideas that survive from prior sweeps keep their id — the ledger then keeps their posted date honest; retired ideas simply drop out of the file (the ledger forgets them).

10 · Integrity machinery

Honest dates — the fingerprint ledger

The build fingerprints each idea's argument (tradeStatement, headline, thesis, changeMyMind, direction, intent, structures, fact texts, pillar scores/notes, levels, earnings result — explicitly excluding chart series, indicator timestamps and computed totals). The committed ledger maps id → {fp, postedAt, updatedAt}: unseen id → both dates = asOf; unchanged fingerprint → both dates carry; changed fingerprint → postedAt carries, updatedAt bumps (a "· updated Nd ago" chip). The build warns when an idea's thesis has gone >10 days unchanged — stale commentary is surfaced, not silently aged. Price/timestamp refreshes alone do not count as change.

The tactical trigger gate

Any idea carrying a levels block must declare a trigger (the stated condition that justifies it now) and a boolean triggered. Only triggered tacticals are written to the output; untriggered ones are dropped and reported ("held back") — so the board never carries a tactical trade without a live justification, and the desk note records what the gate held back (e.g. an EUR/USD call spread waiting for its 1.17 trigger). Tactical options are Growth/appreciation by classification — a directional FX option is not a hedge.

Sourced technicals and charts

tv_technicals.py rewrites the Technical pillar from TradingView's public scanner (SMA50/100/200, Bollinger, RSI, Recommend.MA) direction-aware — a rising trend confirms a long and vetoes a short; neutral/range ideas are scored on tape-calmness instead. It flips only that pillar's dq to sourced; Positioning keeps its authored dq because short interest / CoT aren't in the feed — flipping it wholesale would be dishonest. fetch_chart_series.py fills chart series from Yahoo daily closes with a provenance stamp; the validator strips any chart without one. When a sweep runs without re-running these scripts, the output states the stale as-of honestly rather than pretending freshness.

11 · Supporting engines

  • expressions.js — the "how to express it" knowledge base. Every structure carries: what it is, mechanics, example terms with realistic numbers, pros/cons, a complexity flag (drives the MiFID gate), goal-role scores {g, i, p}, and a context function that personalises the line to the client's actual holding. Structure names are keyword-resolved, so idea structures[] entries must use its vocabulary.
  • email.js — turns any idea × client into a personalised letter: a real-holding hook, a tax-swap / loss-harvest / cash-redeploy / FX-sizing action, concrete implementation terms, a balanced risk line, and a disclosure. Shared by both render paths so copy can't drift.
  • scanner.js — 11 hand-coded, severity-ranked portfolio findings (concentration, underwater tax-loss candidates, idle cash, currency mismatch, missing protection…) powering the Advisor Book agenda and next-best-action; plus thin facades over the mapping engine.
  • morgan.js — the in-app AI assistant; answers methodology questions from window.METHODOLOGY, which reads constants live from the engines so it cannot drift.
  • charts.js — renders the spark/band chart blocks inline on idea cards.

13 · Deployment

  1. GitHub repo (private is fine) → Vercel project via the native Git integration; every push to main auto-deploys the static folder (no build command, output = root).
  2. The one rule: committing locally is invisible — always push. The live site only changes on push.
  3. Commit style: chore: market sweep refresh YYYY-MM-DD (one-line desk summary of what changed) — the git log doubles as the desk's sweep journal.

  3. Sanity-check against the two no-goal clients: Mitch must come out aggressive-growth, Fotis moderate.
  4. Build mapping.js: the four axes exactly as §6, the flat weights, the tradability gate, the goal-alignment multiply, tiers and gates. Verify the canonical worked examples: gold × Amar → Gap fit 0; a collar × Aurora (Retail) → suppressed with reason; five-name sector → concentration raw 80, inverted contribution 20.
  5. Build the pipeline: build_today_focus.py (rubrics as §7 constants, validators as §8–10, ledger, tactical gate, coverage checks), tv_technicals.py, fetch_chart_series.py.

15 · Calibration notes and known limitations

  • Every coefficient (goal weights, comfort pegs, axis weights, tier cut-offs) is a reasoned calibration choice, not a fit to outcomes — they live in PARAMS objects precisely so they are visible and tunable. The in-browser harness (score ideas against window.SEED) is how they were sanity-checked.
  • AUM, currency-hedging depth, liquidity and liability-funding don't yet enter the fit score (scanner.js handles some of this separately).
  • The straddle / short-interest feeds aren't wired, so those pillar inputs stay estimated — which, by design, caps most ideas at Medium until sourced feeds are added. This is a feature: the cap is the visible cost of unsourced data.
  • The sector history is synthetic seed data shaped from position P&L; swap in real monthly history and nothing else changes.
  • The concentration axis's invert-for-fit direction is a product decision on a single flippable flag.
